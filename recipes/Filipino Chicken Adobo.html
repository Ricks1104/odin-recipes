Filipino Chicken Adobo

Chicken adobo is often called the unofficial national dish of the Philippines. It’s a savory, tangy, and slightly sweet braise built on a handful of pantry staples: vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, bay leaves, and black peppercorns. The vinegar was historically used to preserve meat in the tropical climate, and the result is a dish that tastes even better the next day. It’s comforting, deeply flavorful, and almost always served over steamed white rice to soak up the glossy sauce.

The recipe is simple. Chicken pieces, ideally bone-in thighs and drumsticks, are marinated briefly in soy sauce and garlic, then seared to develop color. Vinegar, water, bay leaves, and crushed peppercorns go into the pot, and the chicken simmers until tender. A key technique is to let the vinegar boil for a minute or two before stirring, which mellows its sharp edge. To finish, the chicken is sometimes removed and browned again while the sauce reduces into a rich, slightly sticky glaze. A small amount of brown sugar balances the acidity, though many families leave it out. The whole process takes under an hour, and most of that time is hands-off simmering.

Ingredients

  • 1 kg bone-in chicken thighs and drumsticks
  • ½ cup soy sauce
  • ⅓ cup cane vinegar or white vinegar
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 head garlic, cloves peeled and crushed
  • 3–4 dried bay leaves
  • 1 tsp whole black peppercorns, lightly crushed
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar (optional)
  • 2 tbsp cooking oil
  • Steamed white rice, for serving
  • Sliced spring onions, for garnish (optional)

Here are the steps to make it

  1. Combine the chicken, soy sauce, and half the garlic in a bowl, and marinate for at least 30 minutes (or up to overnight in the fridge).
  2. Remove the chicken from the marinade, pat it dry, and reserve the marinade.
  3. Heat the oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat and sear the chicken skin-side down until golden, about 4–5 minutes per side. Set aside.
  4. In the same pot, sauté the remaining garlic until fragrant and lightly golden, about 1 minute.
  5. Return the chicken to the pot and add the reserved marinade, water, bay leaves, and peppercorns.
  6. Pour in the vinegar and let it come to a boil without stirring for 2 minutes to mellow its sharpness.
  7. Cover, reduce the heat to low, and simmer for 30–35 minutes, until the chicken is tender.
  8. Uncover, stir in the brown sugar if using, and simmer for another 10 minutes until the sauce reduces and thickens slightly.
  9. Discard the bay leaves, garnish with spring onions, and serve hot over steamed rice.
